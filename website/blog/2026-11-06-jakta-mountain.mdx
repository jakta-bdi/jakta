JaKtA started as an acronym, **Jason-like Kotlin Agents**. Only later did we find out that Jakta is also a real
place: a mountain in Norway. Today that mountain is in the background of our home page and inside our logo.
Here is how it got there.

<!-- truncate -->

## From an acronym to a place

The name comes from where the project started: a Kotlin take on [Jason](https://jason-lang.github.io/), the
AgentSpeak interpreter, hence Jason-like Kotlin Agents. We told that story, and how JaKtA 1.x moved away from
Jason, in [Jason-like Kotlin Agents: where JaKtA comes from](/blog/jakta-and-jason).

Then someone searched for the name and found a peak in the Sunnmøre Alps, in the municipality of Ørsta, Norway:
**Jakta**, 1,588 metres high, across the Hjørundfjord from Skårasalen and about 5 kilometres south of Slogen.

## Islands, and then a mountain

It felt like the right kind of coincidence. The languages JaKtA is built on are both named after islands:
Java took its name from coffee from the island of Java, in Indonesia, and Kotlin was named after Kotlin Island,
in the Gulf of Finland near Saint Petersburg, as a nod to Java. A framework born on those two islands could
hardly ask for better than a mountain of its own.

## Climbing the mountain of agent-oriented programming

We also like what a mountain stands for. Agent-oriented programming has a reputation for a steep learning curve:
a new paradigm, often a new language, and new tools to learn before writing the first agent. JaKtA's goal is to
make that climb easier. Agents are plain Kotlin code, written with the language, IDE, build tool and libraries you
already know, so you can start from familiar ground and go up one step at a time.

That is why the peak is in our logo, inside the letters, and why it is the backdrop of the
[home page](/).

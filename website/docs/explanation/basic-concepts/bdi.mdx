# BDI

The philosopher Michael Bratman described humans’ practical reasoning via the “beliefs, desires, intentions” framework,
as a way to explain future-directed decision making[^bratman].
Successively, the framework was formalised by means of modal logics[^rao-georgeff], and then turned into an abstract
semantics for computational agents: AgentSpeak(L)[^agentspeak].

Computational agents are autonomous entities situated into an **environment** they can perceive and affect;
they interact either directly, by exchanging [messages](../interaction/communication.md), or indirectly through the environment,
by means of [stigmergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stigmergy).
The classical implementation of BDI agents, based on the Procedural Reasoning System (PRS)[^prs],
is characterized by four main abstractions, namely:
- **[Beliefs](./beliefs.md)**: a set of facts and rules representing an agent’s epistemic memory, possibly describing its knowledge about the world, itself, and other agents;
- **Desires**: a set of [goals](./goals.md), representing (possibly partial) descriptions of desirable states of the world the agent is willing to achieve, test, or maintain;
- **Intentions**: a set of tasks the agent is currently committed to, in order to satisfy some of its desires;
- **[Plans](./plans.md)**: a set of recipes representing the agent’s procedural memory, hence encoding the know-how about achieving a given intention under certain conditions.

Any instance of the aforementioned abstractions in a BDI agent may vary during its lifetime. For instance:
- novel **beliefs** appear in the agent’s mind whenever it receives novel perceptions from its sensors, while stale beliefs disappear;
- novel **desires** arise when another agent asks for something, or when a plan needs a sub-goal to be accomplished, and they are dropped once achieved or failed;
- novel **intentions** start whenever the agent commits to a desire by selecting a plan for it; an intention grows as its plan pursues sub-goals, and ends when its plan completes or fails.

While carrying on an intention, agents may select one or more **plans** among those supporting the corresponding intention’s accomplishment.
Plans may involve the execution of one or more **actions** – possibly affecting the environment via actuators, which in JaKtA are [skills](../interaction/acting.md) – or the accomplishment of further sub-goals, which may, in turn, require the execution of further plans as part of the same intention.

<details>
<summary>Why BDI implementations have goals instead of desires</summary>

In Bratman’s theory, desires and goals are different things. Desires are whatever the agent would like to be true:
they may be many, and even conflict with each other. Through **deliberation**, the agent picks a consistent subset of
them to pursue, its goals, and commits to some of these by forming intentions.

BDI implementations following PRS, AgentSpeak(L) included, have no explicit representation of desires: they only have
goals. A goal is pursued as soon as it is added, by selecting an applicable plan for it, and that plan becomes (part of)
an intention. Deliberation is thus left to the programmer, who decides *which* goals to add and *when*, and encodes in
plan guards the conditions under which they are worth pursuing.

JaKtA follows the same approach: what the agent wants is expressed as [goals](./goals.md), and intentions are never
written in the program either. They are built by the engine at runtime, out of the plans selected for each goal, as
described in the [execution model](../execution-model.md#intentions-are-coroutines).

</details>
